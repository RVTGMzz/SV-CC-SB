## Presentation/layout reference

The approved presentation style is a polished game-design sheet with **four horizontal boss rows**.

Each row should read left-to-right as:
1. Boss identity / milestone / hero illustration.
2. Phase & skill breakdown.
3. Boss arena concept.
4. Boss Card + ChaCha Boss Form.

Keep the overall Cardcha/Stardew-adventure feeling: readable, charming, magical, colorful, not grimdark, and not visually overloaded.

## Boss I — 20 cards

### Current approved concept
**The Verdant Guardian**

Direction:
- Ancient grove / living ruin guardian.
- Large natural creature made from wood, roots, leaves, stone/arcane core energy.
- This is the first major boss, so it should still feel relatively close to Stardew's natural world.
- Readable, beginner-friendly boss mechanics: melee swings, roots/vines, charge/slam, AoE, limited summons.

### Boss Card
Working concept: **Verdant Core**.

### ChaCha form
Working concept: **Guardian Rabbit**.

Visual rules:
- ChaCha is a **rabbit**, never a cat.
- Preserve ChaCha's compact, round, cute body proportions.
- Both rabbit ears must remain fully readable.
- Transformation should add organic antler/branch/leaf growth and a strong nature silhouette.
- Do not simply dress ChaCha in a cosplay costume.
- Avoid a long torso, heavy humanoid armor, or excessive decorative clutter.

## Boss II — 40 cards

### Current approved concept
**The Hollow Curator**

This boss should stay visually close to the approved reference:
- Tall mysterious hooded archivist/curator.
- Dark navy/black cloak with elegant gold/star-like details.
- Face mostly hidden in shadow with small glowing eyes/light.
- Floating cards / archive-card energy around the boss.
- Library / forgotten archive arena.
- Magical, intelligent, enigmatic rather than monstrous or cute.

Core mechanic direction:
- Observe / scan the player's build.
- Reflect or imitate selected combat patterns.
- Card echoes / clones / mirror effects.
- Arena manipulation and adaptation are more important than raw brute force.

### Boss Card
Working concept: **Mirror Archive**.

### ChaCha form
Working concept: **Mirror Rabbit**.

Visual rules:
- Rabbit identity remains obvious.
- Compact/chibi proportions.
- Dark curator-like magical energy and floating cards are good.
- Keep the cloak/body treatment simple enough that ChaCha still reads as ChaCha transformed, not ChaCha wearing a full costume.
- Both ears remain visible.

## Boss III — 60 cards

### LOCKED core identity
**The Tricolor Resonance** is a **set of three cute elemental/spirit bosses**.

The trio is NOT MiMi and must never accidentally include or resemble MiMi as one of the three.

The three spirits:

### Red — Ignis
Role: aggression / power.
- Red/fire-like spirit.
- Charge / explosive attacks / high damage.

### Green — Vita
Role: life / support/control.
- Green/nature-like spirit.
- Healing / plants or vines / support/buff behavior.

### Blue — Aether
Role: magic / range/control.
- Blue/arcane/water-like spirit.
- Projectiles / teleport / slow / zoning.

Visual rules for all three:
- Cute magical spirits, not cats.
- Small, round, friendly-looking silhouettes even though they are bosses.
- Simple readable shapes with strong red/green/blue identity.
- Their charm is intentional; they should feel collectible/mascot-like rather than intimidating beasts.
- Do not turn them into three recolors of one identical model. Each needs its own elemental silhouette and attack personality.

Fight direction:
- The trio can be fought as a coordinated encounter.
- Later/final phase can combine the three colors/resonances.
- Exact kill-order consequences and balance remain open until combat implementation.

### Boss Card
Working concept: **Tricolor Resonance**.

### ChaCha form
Working concept: **Trinity Rabbit**.

Visual rules:
- ChaCha remains compact, cute, and clearly rabbit-shaped.
- Strong red/green/blue transformation cues are welcome.
- Avoid a long humanoid body.
- Avoid excessive armor/accessories.
- Transformation should alter aura, silhouette, ears/fur/energy effects enough to feel genuinely transformed, not merely dressed in RGB cosplay.

## Boss IV — 80 cards

### LOCKED identity
**MiMi** is the final boss of the first major 80-card Cardcha story arc.

This does NOT mean "MiMi was evil all along". The battle is intended as a story payoff/test/confrontation tied to MiMi's deeper relationship with Cardcha.

### MiMi angelic boss transformation — approved direction
The boss form must still unmistakably be MiMi.

Transformation cue:
1. MiMi begins in her recognizable normal appearance.
2. Her signature hair bow comes loose / falls away.
3. Her hair flows down freely.
4. Her hair keeps **MiMi's original hair color**.
5. She enters a radiant **angelic / celestial resonance form** with wings and stronger Cardcha energy.

Guardrails:
- Preserve MiMi's face, hair color, and recognizable identity.
- Do not redesign her into a generic unrelated angel woman.
- The bow-removal + loose-hair moment should be a memorable visual signal that the real boss form has begun.
- Angelic wings and luminous resonance effects should feel extraordinary compared with her normal NPC design.
- The final design can become grander during later phases, but MiMi herself must remain visually legible.

### Boss Card
Working concept: **MiMi's Resonance**.

### ChaCha final form
Working concept: **Resonance Rabbit**.

Visual rules:
- Rabbit, never cat.
- Cute/compact proportions remain.
- The final form can be the most dramatic transformation, with celestial/radiant wings, halo/resonance motifs, and orbiting cards/energy.
- Even here, avoid an elongated humanoid torso and avoid burying ChaCha under costume parts.
- Transformation should feel like ChaCha's body/resonance has evolved, not like ChaCha put on MiMi-themed clothes.

## Universal ChaCha Boss Form rules — LOCKED visual guardrail

Across Boss I–IV:
- ChaCha is always a **rabbit**.
- Keep the body compact, round, mascot-like, and cute.
- Do not stretch ChaCha into a tall humanoid/chibi-human proportion.
- Keep both ears readable unless a future deliberate story transformation explicitly changes anatomy.
- Boss Forms need a meaningful silhouette/effect transformation, not just clothing/cosplay.
- Keep designs readable at Stardew sprite scale. If a detail will turn into visual noise at pixel scale, simplify it.
- Prefer 2–4 strong transformation motifs over many tiny ornaments.
